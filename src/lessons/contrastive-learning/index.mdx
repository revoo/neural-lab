# Contrastive Learning & Representation Spaces

Word2Vec gave us dense embeddings for **single words**. What if we want one unified vector space for **entire sentences**, **whole photographs**, or even **images and text together** (like OpenAI’s CLIP, where searching `"a golden retriever on a skateboard"` retrieves the exact matching photograph with zero task-specific labels)?

Enter **<Term id="contrastive-learning">contrastive learning</Term>** (SimCLR, MoCo, CLIP, modern sentence-embedding models). The recipe fits on a postcard:

1. Take a mini-batch of $B$ matched **positive pairs** $(\ca{a_i}, \cb{b_i})$ — either two augmented crops of the same photograph (SimCLR), or an image paired with its caption (CLIP).
2. L2-normalise every embedding onto the unit hypersphere ($\|\ca{a_i}\| = \|\cb{b_i}\| = 1$).
3. Treat the other $B - 1$ items in the mini-batch as **in-batch negatives**, and train a $B$-way softmax classifier (**<Term id="infonce">InfoNCE</Term>**) to pick the single true match out of the crowd!

---

## 1. InfoNCE and why temperature $\tau$ mines hard negatives

Divide every pairwise cosine similarity $\ca{a_i} \cdot \cb{b_j}$ by a scalar **temperature** $\cc{\tau} \gt 0$, and compute cross-entropy to pick diagonal match $j = i$:

<Eq
  words="InfoNCE loss  =  cross-entropy of picking the true match i out of all B candidates at temperature τ"
  tex="\mathcal{L}_{\text{InfoNCE}} = -\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i=1}^{B} \log \frac{\exp(\ca{a_i}\cdot\cb{b_i} / \cc{\tau})}{\sum_{j=1}^{B} \exp(\ca{a_i}\cdot\cb{b_j} / \cc{\tau})}"
>
  - $\ca{a_i}, \cb{b_i}$ — L2-normalised embeddings of the $i$-th matching pair (e.g., image $i$ and caption $i$)
  - $\cb{b_j}$ ($j \neq i$) — the $B - 1$ **in-batch negatives** (mismatched captions)
  - $\cc{\tau}$ — **temperature** (`1/τ` is CLIP’s learned logit scale; typically `τ ≈ 0.05–0.15`)
</Eq>

Why does $\cc{\tau}$ matter so dramatically? Because the softmax weight $p_{ij}$ controls **how hard gradient descent pushes each negative away**. When $\cc{\tau}$ is small, an easy negative (`cos = 0.10`) gets almost zero softmax probability, while a **hard negative** (`dog` vs `cat`, `cos = 0.70`) soaks up **nearly 100%** of the repulsive gradient — automatic hard-negative mining built right into cross-entropy!

Slide $\tau$ below and watch the hard negative’s share of repulsive force jump from **49.5%** (`τ = 1.00`) to **100.0%** (`τ = 0.05`).

## 2. Alignment & Uniformity: the two laws of a healthy embedding space

Wang & Isola (2020) proved a beautiful geometric theorem: InfoNCE simultaneously optimises two competing forces on the unit hypersphere (<Term id="alignment-uniformity">Alignment & Uniformity</Term>):

1. **Alignment:** matched pairs $(\ca{a_i}, \cb{b_i})$ should map to the same point ($\mathbb{E}[\|\ca{a_i} - \cb{b_i}\|^2] \to 0$).
2. **Uniformity:** distinct items should spread evenly across the whole hypersphere, preserving maximal information.

## 3. Why contrastive learners crave huge batches (or a MoCo queue)

In-batch InfoNCE only sees negatives that happen to land inside the same mini-batch. If tricky confusable “hard negatives” make up `1%` of your corpus, the probability that a mini-batch of size $B$ includes at least one is $1 - 0.99^{B-1}$:

- At `B = 64`: only **46.9%** chance! More than half your updates never even see a hard negative.
- At `B = 512`: **99.4%** chance — why SimCLR trains with `B = 4,096` and CLIP with `B = 32,768`!
- **MoCo’s clever bypass:** instead of keeping 4,096 full encoder activation graphs in GPU VRAM, maintain a **FIFO queue** of $Q$ precomputed embeddings from a slow-moving momentum encoder. You only backpropagate through a tiny active batch (`B = 64`, **0.63 GiB** VRAM) while still contrasting against thousands of negatives!

<ForReal>

OpenAI's **CLIP** (and open-source **OpenCLIP** / **SigLIP**) learns the temperature as a trainable parameter `logit_scale = nn.Parameter(torch.ones([]) * np.log(1 / 0.07))`, explicitly clamping `logit_scale.exp() <= 100` after every optimiser step to prevent the exact softmax-overflow instability you saw in Brief 2! Meanwhile, modern dense text retrievers (E5, BGE, Contriever) combine in-batch InfoNCE with mined BM25/cross-encoder hard negatives — and **SigLIP** replaces the cross-GPU batch softmax with pairwise sigmoid loss so large-batch training scales cleanly across hundreds of devices.

## 5. InfoNCE temperature sweep in 10 lines of NumPy

```python
import numpy as np

def softmax(x, axis=-1):
    e = np.exp(x - np.max(x, axis=axis, keepdims=True))
    return e / e.sum(axis=axis, keepdims=True)

cos_row = np.array([0.85, 0.70, 0.10, -0.05])  # [true, hard_neg, easy_1, easy_2]
for tau in [1.0, 0.25, 0.10, 0.05]:
    p = softmax(cos_row / tau)
    hard_share = p[1] / p[1:].sum()
    print(f"tau={tau:4.2f} (1/tau={1/tau:4.0f}): P(true)={p[0]:.3f} | hard-neg share={100*hard_share:5.1f}%")
# tau=1.00 (1/tau=   1): P(true)=0.365 | hard-neg share= 49.5%
# tau=0.25 (1/tau=   4): P(true)=0.615 | hard-neg share= 87.7%
# tau=0.10 (1/tau=  10): P(true)=0.817 | hard-neg share= 99.7%
# tau=0.05 (1/tau=  20): P(true)=0.953 | hard-neg share=100.0%
```
